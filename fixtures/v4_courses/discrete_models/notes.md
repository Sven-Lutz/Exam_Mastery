# Synthetic university pilot: discrete retention models
This is explicitly authored development material, not institutional or exam authority.
Learning objectives: derive and apply a constant-retention recurrence; distinguish fractional loss from absolute loss; test the limits of a model against data.
A substance has mass M_n in mg after n full hours. Assume only retention loss, constant retention fraction r=0.8 per hour, and initial M_0=10 mg. The discrete model is M_(n+1)=r*M_n, hence by repeated substitution M_n=M_0*r^n for nonnegative integer n. It is not a continuous-time model. The per-hour fractional loss is 1-r=0.2=20%, but the absolute loss in hour n to n+1 is (1-r)*M_n, so it is not constant. The CSV measurements are synthetic and exactly generated by this model, with no measurement noise. They do not validate any real process.
Worked original exercise: compute M_2. Solution: M_1=10*0.8=8 mg; M_2=8*0.8=6.4 mg. The loss in the first hour is 2 mg and in the second is 1.6 mg.
Transfer exercise: M_0=5 mg, r=0.6. Solution: M_2=5*0.6^2=1.8 mg. A finite list agreeing with a model is not empirical validation, and an intervention that changes retention would invalidate its constant-r assumption.
