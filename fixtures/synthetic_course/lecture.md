# Synthetic course: Earnings per Share and Effective Tax Rate

This is an intentionally constructed DEVELOPMENT FIXTURE, not real lecture evidence.

Learning objectives:
- Define effective tax rate as income tax expense divided by pre-tax profit in this simplified case.
- Compute earnings per share (EPS) as profit after tax divided by weighted average outstanding shares, holding all else fixed.
- Explain the difference between percentage-point reduction in the tax rate and relative increase in EPS.

Simple model: constant pretax profit P, effective tax rate t in the interval [0,1], fixed shares N>0. After-tax profit is P(1-t); EPS = P(1-t)/N.

Synthetic numbers: P=100 monetary units, N=20 million shares, t=0.30.
Then EPS = 100*(1-0.30)/20 = 3.50 monetary units per share. Decreasing t to 0.25 gives 3.75. Difference is +0.25; relative change 0.25/3.5 = 7.142857%.

Boundary: Financial reporting EPS and tax expense are affected by rules, changes in dilutive securities, extraordinary items, and other accounting effects. The toy model ignores these; it is not tax/legal advice.
